# ReclaimBay company charter

## Permanent product direction

ReclaimBay is a **self-service automotive declined-work revenue scanner**.
It helps automotive repair shops inspect their own declined/deferred-work exports,
understand the value and age of the work represented, and identify opportunities
worth reviewing. The scanner is the customer-facing product.

Acquisition, research, outreach and admin infrastructure support marketing and
customer acquisition for the scanner. They are supporting systems, not a separate
product or a reason to redirect the company away from the scanner.

## Primary business objective

**Profitable, recurring, progressively autonomous software revenue.**

Evaluate work by its contribution to customer value, paid adoption, retention,
operating margin and lower recurring manual effort. Discovery counts, eligible
contacts and accepted emails are acquisition activity; they do not establish
scanner adoption, customer value, revenue or profitability.

## Product commitments

- Keep the scanner understandable and usable by a shop without a founder-led
  demonstration or manual report processing.
- Make observations and financial summaries traceable to the supplied report.
  An estimate's declined amount is potential work represented in an export;
  it is not proof of recoverable revenue, realized sales or profit.
- Preserve the existing browser-only report boundary: uploaded files and report
  contents stay in browser memory. Report contents must not enter analytics,
  contact requests, backend records or outreach personalization.
- Keep sample activity distinct from real customer activity. Report measured
  outcomes honestly, including uncertainty and incomplete evidence.
- Pursue recurring customer value and sound economics before expanding the
  machinery used to acquire customers.

## Execution and autonomy

**M1 — Strategic alignment** is complete with the charter, fixed roadmap and
agent guidance recorded, and unfinished replenishment work preserved separately.
The current milestone is **M2 — Commercial readiness of the customer-facing
scanner**, beginning with a local assessment of the existing product.

Follow the [fixed M1–M5 company roadmap](ROADMAP.md). Historical acquisition
implementation labels such as M1, M2 and M3 are not company milestone completions.
The fixed definitions guide sequencing; they do not authorize new features,
scope expansion or operational actions.

Progressive autonomy means automating a demonstrated, bounded process with
evidence, costs, observability, idempotency, stop conditions and an appropriate
authorization boundary. It does not give standing permission to change policies,
deploy, change production, activate acquisition, launch campaigns or send emails.
Existing identity, duplicate, commercial, contact, suppression and protected-record
safeguards remain authoritative.

For the present M2 assessment, do not resume replenishment development,
introduce product features, change acquisition policy or alter production. Preserve
unfinished work separately and keep it out of release inputs. A later task must
explicitly authorize any implementation, deployment or operational action it needs.

## Decision rule

Before proposing work, name the current milestone, the scanner/customer outcome
it serves, the evidence needed to accept it and the smallest useful next step.
Keep hypotheses, implementation, local verification and production validation
distinct. A document or passing local test is not evidence of commercial readiness.

The immediate scanner-readiness task is specified in
[M2 scanner readiness](M2_SCANNER_READINESS.md).
